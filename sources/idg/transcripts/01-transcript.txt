budget, a team, and a deadline. Your director says, "We need AI." Your team says, "We need a super
app." Where do you start? The answer is neither. Great digital products don't start with solutions,
they start with problems. That's what product thinking is about. Welcome to product thinking. This
course isn't about how to be a product manager. It's not about how to build a product. It's about
how to solve a problem. And it's not even about finding answers quickly. It's about asking the right
questions first. The best organizations in the world, they don't just build things. They build the
right things. As product officers, our job is to solve problems. Increasingly, that involves
building digital products. But our role isn't just to build the product ourselves. It's to create
the conditions for our teams to succeed. Three things help us do that. First, focus on the problem,
not the solution. "We need more AI." "We need a super app." These are solutions dressed up as
problems. The more useful questions are, "Who are our users? What are they trying to do? And what is
getting in their way?" Second, chase outcomes, not outputs. A website with five buttons is an
output. More citizens completing transactions is an outcome. Chase the outcome. Third, integrate
policy, ops, and tech. No initiative succeeds when these three work in silos. Let's take a look at
how the world's more successful organizations put this into practice. Grab started with one single
goal: to make taxis safer, more reliable, and easier to book. Today, it's a super app, but it didn't
start there. Amazon started with books, one category, and more accessible and more affordable.
Today, it's the everything store. Netflix started by making DVD rentals cheaper and more reliable.
Today, it runs sophisticated recommendation algorithms. But the foundation was in technology. It was
superior operations. The pattern is the same every time. Solve one problem first, then scale. The
best organizations in the world solve problems, they don't just build solutions. We in the public
service can do the same. This course covers how we diagnose why problems happen, we will craft a
sharp problem statement, we find the right metrics, assumptions, and manage risks. Let's begin
starting with the one.
